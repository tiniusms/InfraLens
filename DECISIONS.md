## 2026-10-14 - Annual electricity cost is an energy-charge identity
**Decision:** The first financial translation is a tested annual energy-cost identity: facility demand MW = IT capacity MW × utilization × PUE; kWh = demand MW × 1000 × hours; USD = kWh × cents/kWh / 100. PUE must be at least 1. Hours default to 8760, and any other positive hour count is a scenario assumption rather than a calendar lookup. Several named prices can be compared against one baseline on the same load. Model-derived EIA prices must carry the frozen 60-case holdout FAIL warning, and a future demo must set that flag automatically. The calculator does not add PJM capacity prices, demand charges, or a discount rate, and it does not read the screening layer or refit Ridge.
**Evidence:** 50 MW IT, PUE 1.30, utilization 0.80, 8760 hours, and 12 cents/kWh produce 52 MW average demand, 455,520,000 kWh, and $54,662,400. The holdout primary criterion remains FAIL, with both Ridge and the seasonal-naive baseline underpredicting and empirical interval coverage at 0%.
**What this decision does not do:** It does not change the EIA target, features, holdout, or PJM screening rules. It does not implement a DCF, a tariff engine, or a risk score. It does not treat a Ridge price as a validated investment forecast.

## 2026-09-29 - Ridge is the development-selected EIA candidate; model class and features are frozen

## 2026-09-28 - EIA price layer stays separate from PJM zonal screening
**Decision:** Version 1 has two layers. EIA remains the state-level monthly commercial-price forecasting layer (unchanged target, m+3 availability, development window, and reserved holdout). PJM Long-Term Load Forecast data is a **zonal screening/risk layer only**. Do not use PJM zonal indicators as state-level ML features. Do not aggregate PJM zones to VA, PA, OH, MD, or NJ for modeling. Do not use Data Miner. Dashboard MVP ingest, when implemented, is public 2024–2026 LTLF files only. If InfraLens stores a revised artifact, `vintage_available_at` is the revision date (2024 revised LTLF → **2024-02-01**), not the original LAS listing (**2023-12-27** for 2024).
**Evidence:** (1) Official LTLF geography is transmission-owner zone, LDA, and RTO, not state ([2026 Load Forecast Report](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/library/reports-notices/load-forecast/2026-load-report.pdf)). (2) Several zones that overlap the five user-facing states also cover other states or DC (AEP, APS, DOM, DPL, PEPCO, DEOK, RECO, OVEC). (3) Dated public vintages are annual from about 2016/2020 onward; they do not cover the EIA development origins that start 2002-01-31. (4) Forcing zonal peaks or CAGRs into the state price model would invent a geographic allocator and a vintage-fill rule the evidence does not support. (5) Zonal summer-peak growth and large-load / data-center adjustments still answer the analyst screening question (where PJM is embedding data-center-driven peak risk) without touching the price model. Original-vs-revision: [LAS](https://www.pjm.com/committees-and-groups/subcommittees/las) lists 2024 LTLF at 2023-12-27; the library PDF is marked Revised 2024-02-01; Inside Lines figures from 2024-01-08 do not match the revised PDF.
**Alternatives considered:** Map each zone to a primary state and use 10-year CAGR as an EIA feature (rejected: cross-border zones). Allocate zonal MW by load share or area (rejected for V1: no tested allocator). Ingest every historical LTLF from 2016 for a zonal backtest aligned to EIA origins (rejected: still cannot fill 2002–2015; not needed for dashboard MVP). Use Data Miner for monthly zonal load (rejected: redistribution terms).
**Impact:** No change to ML target, user-facing geography, success criteria, EIA availability rule, or holdout. PJM work is screening-dataset design next, not modeling. Inventory notes: `docs/pjm_ltlf_inventory.md`. Schema and display-only zone/state table: `docs/pjm_screening_schema.md`.
**Revisit trigger:** A tested, leakage-safe zonal-to-state rule is published and historical LTLF coverage is long enough to justify a separate experiment—without using Data Miner or the reserved EIA holdout.

## 2026-09-14 - Publication-lag-constrained current-vintage evaluation
**Decision:** For EIA month *m*, set `assumed_available_at` to the first day of month *m+3*. Forecast origin is the analyst decision date. The primary target is commercial price in the calendar month 12 months after that origin. Every input at an origin must satisfy `assumed_available_at <= forecast_origin`. The baseline is availability-adjusted seasonal-naive: the most recent same-calendar-month observation that was available at the origin—not an unavailable t-12. This backtest is a **publication-lag-constrained current-vintage** evaluation: API history may already include later revisions and is not a real-time vintage replay.
**Evidence:** EIA API rows have no publication/revision timestamp. EPM Table 5.6.A currently lags the data month by about two months (e.g. June 2026 released 2026-08-26). Form EIA-861M/826 data are due at the end of the following month. A three-month lag to the *first day of m+3* is conservative relative to that schedule.
**Alternatives considered:** Use `period` as availability (rejected: leakage). Use a two-month lag (less conservative). Wait for a full historical vintage calendar before any baseline (blocks the capstone). Pure t-12 on observation time (would use June 2024 at a 2024-06-30 origin).
**Impact:** Locks the first evaluation convention. Does not change the ML target (12-month-ahead commercial price), geography, or success metric (MAE vs this baseline). Reserves target months 2025-07 through 2026-06 as the final holdout until model selection.
**Revisit trigger:** An official month-by-month EPM/API release calendar or vintage file shows that m+3 is too strict or too loose.
